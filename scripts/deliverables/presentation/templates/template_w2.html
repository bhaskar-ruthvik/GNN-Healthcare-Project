How the comparisons are kept fair

Every model ranks the same candidates for the same referrals, and every test was written down before running

✓
Same referrals: first-time referrals in 2018, each ranked against the same eligible specialists (same specialty, within 25 miles, new to the patient)
✓
Same training examples and information: every model learns from the same past referrals and sees only what was known before each referral
✓
Same tuning: each model tuned on 2018 only, by the same rule the graph model was tuned by
✓
Decided in advance: a 2-point margin in “chosen specialist in top 5” counts as a real difference; 2019 is used once, to confirm

Main measure: how often the specialist the PCP actually chose is among the model’s top 5 (“top-5 match”).

Also shown: share of the top 5 that are new to the PCP · share of top picks going to the busiest 10% of specialists · median travel · MIPS gain with a 30% quality weight.

3 · Four lenses

At equal quality, the graph model eases congestion

2018 first-time referrals, same candidates; with the 30% quality weight where stated

Fit = the specialist the PCP chose is in our 5 · Quality = MIPS points vs the chosen specialist · Travel = median miles from the patient · Load = top picks going to the busiest 10% (actual referrals 29.9%) · New = share of the 5 the PCP has never used.

Reading. On fit and quality the two are level. The graph model eases congestion: about 6 points fewer top picks on the busiest specialists. The table proposes more new connections and slightly shorter trips.

Confirmed on 2019 (used once): load −5.4 points [−7.3, −3.4]; quality +0.5 (n.s.); fit +2.1 points; new connections −11 points.

What the table relies on: specialist attributes (), distance (), diagnosis (). The graph model leans on who the PCP referred to before — history that mostly does not recur (next slide).

So: the graph model is not yet using the network to make better connections than a table can. That is what the architecture work targets.

4 · Across groups

In almost every group, the graph model eases congestion

Graph model − table, with the 30% quality weight; 2018 first-time referrals. Intervals resample whole PCPs

Reading. The graph model sends fewer top picks to the busiest 10% of specialists than the table in every group large enough to judge — by patient age, access, PCP tenure and network, and the specialist’s tenure, experience and location — while its MIPS gain is equal or higher.

Hollow markers: fewer than 100 patients (not judged). Groups are defined from data only (no outside downloads): access = distance to the 5th-nearest eligible specialist; tenure = years enrolled in Medicare.

5 · What the table does differently

The table’s extra new connections often lack history with the patient’s insurer

2018, with the 30% quality weight. Where the table recommends specialists new to the PCP that the graph model does not, compared with what the graph model recommends instead

Reading. Slightly higher MIPS and closer, but often never seen the patient’s insurer. The graph model knows these relationships from the network, so its new connections are fewer but more feasible.

Inside both models. MIPS carries little weight ( of the table, ≈1% of the graph model). Both imitate PCP choices, which are MIPS-neutral; the MIPS gain comes from the 30% quality weight.

Next model change: the objective. Train toward better-MIPS, less-congested specialists, keeping the graph’s insurer and condition fit.

Discussion

Questions for you

Decisions that shape the next experiments

1
Next model change: the training objective. Both models are trained to imitate PCP choices, which are MIPS-neutral. Proposal: train toward better-MIPS, less-congested specialists while keeping the insurer and condition fit the graph provides. Agree with this direction?
2
Pitch framing. Graph models are more data-efficient and use new referrals without retraining, but the current one plateaus. Is “the relational structure matters; the current architecture under-uses it” the right story?
3
Data on TAMUS VISION. Does the approval for the prepared (never raw) inputs cover VISION as well as Grace?
4
Patient rurality and career stage. The data has rural/urban codes only for doctors, and only years enrolled in Medicare (one fixed number per doctor, piling up at 15). OK to add the public USDA ZIP rural codes and graduation year from Medicare’s doctor directory (by NPI)?
