## Inside "specialist attributes" (the professor's question)
No single attribute dominates; the 24% is spread thin:
- specialty 4.8%, credentials 2.2% — both candidates share the specialty, so its share reflects interactions with other inputs;
- **patient-panel composition** (what kind of patients the specialist sees: share 65–74, male, under 65, average risk, average age, 75–84, 85+, female) **≈ 10% combined** — the largest specialist block, plausibly a proxy for sub-specialty / case-mix fit;
- volume and tenure (Medicare patients 1.4%, years in Medicare 1.3%, practice size 1.1%, services billed 0.6%) ≈ 4%;
- **MIPS 1.3%**; online rating 0.7%, number of ratings 1.0%.
Patient–specialist prior visits contribute 0 by construction (first-time referrals: neither candidate was seen by the patient).

## Reading
The model's choice is driven by the PCP's referral habits and geography, then by the diagnosis; specialist quality signals (MIPS, ratings) carry ~2–3% combined. This matches 10.4 (removing PCP history changes the lists most; removing MIPS changes nothing) and explains why MIPS gains require explicitly blending MIPS into the ranking. Caveats: correlated inputs (volume, practice size, panel counts) share credit unstably; one seed (seed 1 pending as a stability check); 60 referrals.
