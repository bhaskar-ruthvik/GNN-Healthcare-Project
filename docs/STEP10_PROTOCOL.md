Follows the professor meeting of 2026-09-25 (PROJECT_LOG 2026-09-28). Replaces MRR as the criterion for model and analysis decisions. Draft 1 and its audit: `reviews/STEP10_PROTOCOL_draft1_20260928.md`, PROJECT_LOG 2026-09-28. Edited after freezing only by dated amendment.

Step numbering used everywhere (log, protocol, results): **10.0** protocol · **10.1** yardstick table + descriptive charts · **10.2** baseline on the yardstick (v4 checkpoints) · **10.3** v5 inputs + retraining · **10.4** filters and reliance ablations · **10.5** bias · **10.6** per-input SHAP · **10.7** load-aware recommendation (later).

## 1. Question and decision rule
Does the recommender propose specialists (connections) with higher MIPS than the specialist actually referred to, while staying consistent with the case? Where do those connections come from, what do they cost in travel, and how do they spread load? The observed referral is the comparison point, not assumed optimal.

**Primary result = a frontier, not a single number.** For each scorer family and setting: x-axis **fidelity** = R@5: the share of delivered 5-lists that contain the referred specialist; y-axis **MIPS gain** (top-5, conservative bound, §2.4). Fidelity, MIPS gain and distance are all measured on the same delivered 5-list. Distance from the patient and load are co-reported for every point.

**Admissibility rule (pre-registered; applied on validation only).** Every recommendation already satisfies the 25-mile cap (§2.2 rule 4). A configuration is admissible if (a) validation fidelity (R@5) ≥ PCP habit's validation R@5, and (b) its recommendations are no farther from patients than current practice: median and p90 of recommended distance (top-5 slots) ≤ the median and p90 of the referred specialist's distance on the same referrals (user decision 2026-09-28: distance is judged from the patient, never relative to the referred specialist). Among admissible configurations, the chosen one maximises validation MIPS gain (conservative bound). Checkpoints are never selected on MIPS. **"No admissible quality-aware configuration" is a valid, reportable result** (on 09-25, the model's top-10 re-sort kept the referred specialist 29% of the time vs PCP habit's 36%, so the rule may admit only the unmodified list); the frontier is reported in full regardless. The p90 part of (b) is weak under the 25-mile cap; top-1 distance, p75 and the share beyond 10 mi are reported descriptively (no further conditions without the user).

**Primary contrast (one).** The chosen configuration (whatever scorer family it comes from) vs current practice on MIPS gain. Also reported: the best admissible **model** configuration, and the **graph contribution** = best admissible model configuration − best admissible heuristic configuration (PCP habit or distance re-sorts), paired CI. Matched-fidelity comparisons show the neighbouring grid points on both sides. Everything else is exploratory and reported in full.

## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Cohort and exclusion order (counts reported at each step; identical query set for every scorer)
First-time referrals (no earlier visit of the patient to that specialist). Exclusions, in order:
1. Referred specialist's specialty unknown (`missing`).
2. **Non-choice specialties** (user decision 2026-09-28: excluded; applied to the referred specialist's specialty read strictly before the referral, not the last label ever): facility-based services the PCP does not choose a named doctor for — diagnostic radiology, interventional radiology, nuclear medicine, pathology, emergency medicine, anesthesiology, CRNA, hospitalist, critical care, ambulatory surgical center, radiation therapy centers (24.5% of all referral events). **Non-specific labels** — internal medicine, family practice, general practice, nurse practitioner, physician assistant, clinical nurse specialist, single/multispecialty group (13.4%). Both are reported as separate secondary tables, not in the primary analysis.
3. Referred specialist inactive under §2.2 rule 2 (share reported; these referrals stay in a sensitivity table with ever-active).
4. Referred specialist's MIPS is zero/imputed (no reference value; reported separately with the share).
5. Eligible set (before the distance cap) smaller than 5.
6. Missing or unmapped patient ZIP: excluded from the primary analysis (no cap or distance can be applied); count reported; secondary table.
`mips_rerank.py`'s rules (drop referrals whose target is outside the pool; ≥ 10 scored candidates) are replaced by this list.

### 2.2 "Fits the case" — the eligible set for each referral
A specialist is eligible if **all** hold:
1. **Same specialty** as the referred specialist, both read **strictly before** the referral date (`specialty_asof` currently reads on-or-before; changed for this step and checked).
2. **Active**: at least one claim in the **365 days** before the referral date (sensitivity: ever active).
3. **New to the patient.**
4. **Distance from the patient ≤ 25 miles** (patient ZIP centroid to specialist location; 86% of actual first-time referrals fall within it). If n < 5 eligible specialists lie within 25 mi (≈ 15% of referrals), the eligible set is the n within the cap plus the nearest (5 − n) beyond it (fill to exactly 5). Filled referrals **stay in the primary analysis** and are also shown separately; for them every scorer's top-5 set, gain and R@5 are identical and only top-1 differs. (Missing ZIP: excluded, §2.1 step 6.) Caps of 10 and 50 mi and no cap are the 10.4 sweep. The referred specialist need not be inside the cap. MIPS-sorted breaks ties by distance.
**Secondary eligible set:** the above ∩ specialists with prior claims under the patient's insurer (as-of) — the feasibility check.
**Reported plausibility shares for every list:** prior insurer history; prior encounters in the query's disease category (as-of); new to the PCP.

### 2.3 MIPS
Raw points (0–100); no percentiles in reported results. Zeros are "no MIPS score (0 in source)"; the professor treats them as new doctors, footnoted with the tenure contradiction (§7).
**Model input (v5, as-of):** for each specialist on each day, the mean of the distinct **positive** values seen strictly before that day; flags `mips_none` (only zeros seen so far; value imputed with the specialty mean of positive values over specialists active in the training period), `mips_multi` (> 1 positive distinct value), `mips_partial_zero` (a zero alongside positive values); input `mips_range` (max − min of positive values, 0 if single).
**Yardstick (evaluation):** one fixed value per specialist = mean of all distinct positive values, applied identically to the referred specialist and every candidate. Scorers never rank on the yardstick; MIPS-sorted ranks on the as-of input value.
**Bounds:** *conservative* = recommended at their minimum distinct value, referred at their maximum, imputed recommended = 0; *generous* = the reverse, imputed at the specialty mean. A gain is claimed only if it holds under the conservative bound. The point estimate uses means with imputed recommended at the specialty mean. The conservative bound slightly favours single-valued specialists (no spread); accepted as uncertainty-averse.

### 2.4 Metrics (per scorer, per split)
**Quality.** MIPS gain top-5 (mean of top-5 − referred) and top-1, in points: mean, median and a histogram of per-referral gain. Share of top-1 better / equal / worse than referred (imputed slots count as not better). Stratified by referred MIPS band (< 50, 50–< 90, 90–< 100, = 100) and by `mips_multi`. **Low-MIPS avoidance**: share of top-1 below the training-period p10 of its specialty. **Headroom captured**: mean gain ÷ mean headroom (best eligible MIPS − referred MIPS), over referrals with headroom > 0 (ratio of means, not mean of ratios).
**Connections.** New-to-PCP share of top-5 slots (reference: the referred specialist's new-to-PCP share, ≈ 36%). Good new connections: slots new to the PCP and better than referred. Share of slots on `mips_none` specialists.
**Distance (from the patient).** (i) Distance to each recommended specialist (top-1; top-5 slots): median, p90, histogram, shares beyond 10 / 25 mi; the same for the referred specialist on the same referrals. (ii) **Excess over nearest** = recommended distance − distance to the nearest eligible specialist (median, p90); the referred specialist's excess is the reference (training sample: median 4.5, p90 20.7 mi). No metric is defined relative to the referred specialist's distance. Missing ZIP → excluded from distance metrics.
**Load (within specialty).** Top-1 recommendations compared with actual referrals, one per referral; top-5 as slot shares (1/5 per slot). Gini **within each specialty** over its eligible specialists (zeros included), referral-weighted average across specialties. Share of recommendations to specialists above their specialty's training-period p90 first-time volume. **Congestion ratio** per specialist = (their share of their specialty's recommendations) ÷ (their share of the specialty's training-period first-time referrals), both with +1 smoothing on counts; median and p90 across specialists; specialists with no training-period load reported as their own row (the same definition is reused for 10.7). Number of distinct specialists recommended.
**Fidelity.** R@5 of the delivered list (frontier axis); MRR and R@10 reported for continuity.

### 2.5 Scorers
1. **Current practice** (reference row).
2. **PCP habit** (ties by distance, then fixed-seed random).
3. **Random**: exact expectation (top-5 mean = eligible-set mean; share better = share of the eligible set above the referred specialist).
4. **MIPS-sorted** on the as-of input value, ties by distance.
5. **Distance-sorted** (nearest first; the minimum-distance reference).
6. **Final model** — v4 masked (seeds 42, 1) and v4 unmasked (seeds 42, 1) in 10.2; v5 masked (seeds 42, 1) plus a same-seed duplicate `v5_masked_s42_dup` (the nondeterminism band for MIPS metrics) in 10.3; v5 unmasked (seed 42) for 10.5 only.
7. **Quality-aware re-sorts** for scorers 2, 5 and 6, each producing a **full ranking**: the scorer's top K within the eligible set re-sorted by as-of MIPS (ties by the scorer's own order), followed by the rest in the scorer's own order; K ∈ {5, 6, 7, 8, 10, 20, 50, all}. K = 5 delivers the scorer's own 5-list reordered. At K = all the distance re-sort equals scorer 4; the model and PCP-habit re-sorts differ from scorer 4 through tie-breaking at the 100-point ceiling (reported; informative).
8. **Blends** for scorers 2 and 6: rank by (1 − λ)·z(scorer score) + λ·z(as-of MIPS), z computed within the eligible set, λ ∈ {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.5} — intermediate fidelity points between the base list and the re-sorts.
The **final configuration** for 10.4–10.6 is named in the decision record (§3) before any test output.

## 3. Splits, test procedure, uncertainty
- **Two passes.** Pass 1 produces **validation-only** tables. The choice (scorer family, K or λ; from 10.3, v4 vs v5 inputs) is written to the versioned decision file `runs/analysis/step10_decisions.json`, one timestamped entry per step and run set, and logged. Pass 2 produces the test column; the analysis script refuses `--split test` unless an entry exists for the exact step and runs being evaluated, and records that entry's hash in the output. The primary 25-mile cap is fixed by §2.2 (changing it needs an amendment). Before 10.3 produces any v5 test column, a validation-only v4-vs-v5 choice is recorded. Any later change is a dated amendment. Every final table shows validation and test side by side.
- Validation = all 2018 first-time referrals after §2.1 exclusions. The 3,000 queries used for checkpoint selection are included; results excluding them are reported as a sensitivity. The 2019 test year was inspected once on 2026-09-25; disclosed on every test table.
- Uncertainty: patient-cluster bootstrap, 1,000 resamples, paired between scorers; PCP-cluster bootstrap as a sensitivity (119 PCPs). CIs only, no p-values. Two seeds per learned model plus a same-seed nondeterminism band for the MIPS metrics (one duplicated run); differences below max(band, seed gap) are not attributed. Masking attribution uses the v4 pairs (two seeds each); v5 masked vs unmasked (one unmasked seed) is descriptive only. Bias groups: ≥ 100 patients.

## 4. Order of work
1. **10.0** Reviewer concurrence → FREEZE.
2. **10.1** Fixed per-specialist yardstick table (aggregate checks only) and descriptive charts. Charts are aggregate: binned/hexbin plots, specialties with < 11 specialists suppressed, no provider identifiers. 3. **10.2** Scorers 1–7 on v4 checkpoints with the yardstick; pass 1 (validation), decision record, pass 2 (test). `mips_rerank.py` updated (exclusions, yardstick source, bounds) or replaced.
4. **10.3** v5 inputs (§2.3 as-of rules; other attributes keep the existing ambiguity rule) with checks; retrain v5 masked (s42, s1) and unmasked (s42); checkpoint selection unchanged (first-time MRR); re-run 10.2 scorers.
5. **10.4** Candidate filters (distance caps 10 / 50 mi / none vs the primary 25 mi; insurer set) and **reliance diagnostics** (inference-time removal of MIPS, PCP history, affiliation, insurer history, distance). Reliance diagnostics describe the model; they are not configurations to keep. Keeping or dropping an input requires retraining and a decision-record entry.
6. **10.5** Bias: §2.4 metrics by patient gender, race, age band, insurer, area (patient ZIP3; cells < 100 patients suppressed); group gap in practice vs in recommendations; masked vs unmasked.
7. **10.6** Per-input SHAP on the final configuration. One input = the attribute's value, missing and ambiguity columns together (~60 groups, ≈ 4× the 14-group cost). Explained quantity: model score of the top-1 minus the score of the referred specialist. CIs reported; correlated inputs (volume, practice size, panel counts) flagged as unstable. SHAP explains the base model's score, not the MIPS re-sort or blend.
8. **10.7** Load-aware recommendation, only after 10.2 reports the baseline load profile.

## 5. Expected result stated in advance
The base model imitates observed referrals; its top-5 was slightly below the referred specialist's MIPS on 09-25. A MIPS gain is not expected from the base model; it is expected from quality-aware re-sorting among plausible candidates, with a cost in fidelity and possibly travel. The frontier is the result; finding no admissible quality-aware configuration is a valid outcome.

## 6. Caveats stated in every table
Higher MIPS is an allocation property, not proven clinical superiority. MIPS is a single snapshot of unknown year applied across 2015–2019; gaps near the ceiling (95 vs 100) are not equivalent to gaps in the tail — interpret by band. Multi-valued MIPS is averaged over practices whose scores differ by a median of 29 points; the referral's own practice score is not used (symmetry with candidates). Distances use patient ZIP centroids, not addresses. Zero-MIPS specialists are labelled "no MIPS score"; the professor's "new doctor" reading is contradicted by tenure. Race is computed/imputed. Patient ZIP is fixed per patient; 5% of rows disagree with the recorded distance by > 20 mi. Test year previously inspected.
